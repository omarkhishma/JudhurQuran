# 04 — Competitive analysis: Quranic roots, word study, concordance and tafsir products

Date of research: 2026-10-02. Scope: every site named in the brief plus the Arabic-language sites that actually rank for Arabic root and word-meaning queries, plus every Quran root-graph visualizer found. Method: real pages were rendered in headless Chrome at 1366×900 (desktop) and 390×844 with an iPhone user agent (phone), screenshots were read, page text and network requests were captured, raw server HTML was fetched with curl to test indexability, and SERPs were sampled with a web-search tool. Every number below was read off a live page or computed from a live page on this date unless marked otherwise.

## 0. Bottom line

- Nobody combines the four things this product is built on: an Arabic-first UI, verified root/lemma/pattern data with visible provenance, verse-level tafsir in the same flow, and an interactive root-family graph. Each competitor has one or two of the four.
- The strongest data products (corpus.quran.com, quranmorphology.com, quranwbw.com) are English-first. The strongest Arabic product (tafsir.app, "الباحث القرآني") has excellent sources but no graph, no plain-language layer, and its root index is a dense modal.
- quran.com, the biggest Quran site, still shows only translation and transliteration when you click a word (verified in its January 2026 Study Mode). No root, no lemma, no count. That is the largest single gap in the market.
- The same question gets different answers across the web. Root ع ل م is reported as 854, 772, 728, 856, "more than 750", or 582 depending on the site. Nobody explains what they counted. A precise, defined, sourced count is an SEO and trust opportunity on its own.
- Arabic SERPs for "جذر كلمة", "معنى كلمة … في القرآن", "كم مرة ذكرت", "مشتقات", "وزن كلمة" are dominated by almaany.com, ad-heavy content sites (surahquran.com, quran-words.com), static tafsir pages (quran.ksu.edu.sa), and Q&A farms that quote conflicting numbers. The best data sites are invisible because they are client-rendered SPAs.
- Graph visualization of Quran vocabulary exists only as (a) a static PNG ontology map on corpus.quran.com, (b) Quran-only topic mind maps on puremonotheism.com, and (c) Sankey/radial charts on quranobservatory.org with unreadably small Arabic. Nobody offers a root → lemma → word → verse node canvas with readable cards, and nobody lets users build, publish, and fork their own graphs.
- AI-generated roots are already shipping (jadhr.app, whose terms say outputs "are generated by language and speech models and may contain errors"; our lookup returned "AI credits exhausted."). A 2026 peer-reviewed benchmark measured LLM root accuracy on Quranic words at 21.6% (GPT-4o) and 44.1% (LLaMA-4). Verified, cited data is a real differentiator, not a nicety.
- qurancorpus.app, named in the brief as "the new Quranic Arabic Corpus", is dead: the domain now redirects to a "Ce domaine est à vendre" (domain for sale) page, and the v2 repository's last push was 2023-07-21.

## 1. Method and evidence notes

- Headless renders: puppeteer-core driving the locally installed Google Chrome with a throwaway profile, 4–15 s settle time per page; desktop and phone screenshots captured for each primary page and read.
- Indexability test: raw HTML fetched with curl (no JavaScript), scripts and styles stripped, Arabic characters counted. Zero means a crawler that does not execute JavaScript sees no Arabic content.
- SERPs: sampled with a US-located web-search tool on 2026-10-02. Real rankings in Saudi Arabia, Egypt and the Gulf can differ; treat ranking claims as directional and confirm with Search Console and a MENA keyword tool once the site exists.
- almaany.com returned a Cloudflare "Just a moment… Performing security verification" page (HTTP 403) to curl, WebFetch and headless Chrome. We did not try to bypass it. almaany is analyzed from its SERP titles, URLs and snippets only.
- quran.com word click was tested by hovering and clicking word 2:255:27 (يَعْلَمُ) and opening the Study Mode panel.
- Counts for root ع ل م were cross-checked against corpus.quran.com's own page (854 occurrences; 728 distinct verses computed from the 854 listed locations).

## 2. Competitor profiles

### 2.1 quran.com (Quran Foundation) — plus QUL and the Quran Foundation API

What it is: the largest Quran reading site. Next.js, server-rendered, 19 UI languages, Arabic at /ar.

Does well:
- Reading experience is the benchmark: page-accurate King Fahd Complex glyph fonts (the page-42 font file is literally named "p42-v2"), clean dark/light themes, excellent phone layout, verse-by-verse and mushaf reading modes, and a "Translation Reading Mode" added 2026-01-16.
- Word hover shows an English gloss tooltip; click opens Study Mode (launched 2026-01-22) with word translation, transliteration and pronunciation audio, plus tabs for Tafsirs, Lessons, Reflections, Answers, Hadith (added 2026-02-10) and Related Content.
- Verse audio with word-by-word sync highlighting; word-level pronunciation audio.
- Many tafsirs in Arabic and English with clean, canonical URLs such as /ar/al-baqarah/255/tafsirs/ar-tafsir-muyassar.
- SEO is the best in class: named slugs (/ayatul-kursi), hreflang for 6+ locales on each page, JSON-LD (Organization, WebSite, WebPage, BreadcrumbList, Article for tafsir), per-verse meta descriptions.
- Personal layer: notes, reflections, collections (2026-01-31), reading bookmarks, learning plans; sharing and copy buttons on every verse.
- Diacritic-insensitive search: "يعلمون" returned 143 results including يَعْلَمُونَ and يُعَلِّمُونَ.

Missing or frustrating:
- No morphology at all on the word panel. Study Mode for 2:255:27 showed exactly two fields: "Translation: He knows" and "Transliteration: yaʿlamu". No root, lemma, pattern, part of speech, or occurrence count, and no way to jump to other verses with the same root.
- Search conflates forms by letters only (the يعلمون search returns the form-II verb يُعَلِّمُونَ in 2:102) and has no root search.
- The rendered Quran text is glyph codes in a page font, not Unicode: the page text contains private-use characters such as "ﲓ ﲔ ﲕ". Copy works through their copy button, but selecting the text natively does not give real Arabic.
- The visually hidden H1 used for SEO and screen readers is naively normalized and misspelled: "الله لا الاه الا هو … ولا ييوده حفظهما". A screen reader reads "الاه" and "ييوده".
- No graph, no concordance.

Data source: Quran Foundation / Tarteel's Quranic Universal Library (QUL).

API and downloads: the Quran Foundation Content API needs registered app credentials ("Register your app to receive pre-live credentials"); the User API needs OAuth. QUL (qul.tarteel.ai) publishes downloads, not an API ("no API is available"): 227 translations (22 word-by-word), 115 tafsirs, 131 recitations of which 58 have word-timing segments, 28 mushaf layouts, 24 fonts, 77,432 morphology entries (grammar, roots, lemmas, stems), 5,277 mutashabihat phrases, 4,001 similar-ayah sets, 2,512 topics, in SQLite and JSON.

### 2.2 corpus.quran.com (Quranic Arabic Corpus, QAC)

What it is: the University of Leeds academic corpus (Kais Dukes, 2009–2017), now footered "Maintained by the quran.com team".

Does well:
- The deepest free morphology: every word segmented and tagged (POS, person, gender, number, case, mood, verb form, root, lemma), with an auto-generated English sentence per word, for example: "The 27th word of verse (2:255) is an imperfect verb (فعل مضارع). … The verb's triliteral root is ʿayn lām mīm (ع ل م)."
- Quran Dictionary by root with a summary and a concordance: "The triliteral root ʿayn lām mīm (ع ل م) occurs 854 times in the Quran, in 14 derived forms", then every occurrence grouped by form, with location, transliteration, gloss, and the verse fragment with the word in red (true KWIC).
- Verb concordance, lemma frequency list, morphological search, syntactic dependency treebank, ontology of concepts, per-word message board for corrections.
- Server-rendered JSP pages with stable URLs per word location (/wordmorphology.jsp?location=(2:255:27)) and per root (/qurandictionary.jsp?q=Elm). The project README says it ranks first on Google for "Quran Grammar", "Quran Syntax", "Quran Dictionary".

Missing or frustrating:
- Desktop-only: no viewport meta tag; on a 390 px phone the page lays out at 992 px and is shrunk to unreadable size.
- On the word-by-word page each Arabic word is an image (/wordimage?id=5174), not selectable text.
- English UI only (Arabic grammar terms appear as secondary labels); no tafsir; verse audio only (Saad Al-Ghamadi), no word audio or highlighting.
- Treebank covers about half the Quran (the v2 README: "Completing the remaining 50% of grammar diagrams"); the ontology "visual" is a static PNG image map (/images/ontology/map/allah.png).
- Design dates from 2009; development effectively stopped (copyright 2009–2017, v2 repo idle since 2023).
- Root letters are hidden behind Buckwalter codes in URLs (q=Elm), which are meaningless to Arabic users.

Data source: its own annotation (AI-generated, then human-reviewed), Quran text from Tanzil, seven English translations.

API and downloads: morphology file v0.4 downloadable after entering an email; GPL with the clause "Permission is granted to copy and distribute verbatim copies of this file, but CHANGING IT IS NOT ALLOWED", attribution link required. A Java API library; no REST API.

### 2.3 qurancorpus.app, QAC v2, and the "Corpus Quran" apps

- qurancorpus.app now returns HTTP 301 to ktdrain.carrd.co, a page titled "Ce domaine est à vendre" (this domain is for sale). The new corpus site no longer exists.
- github.com/kaisdukes/quranic-corpus ("Quranic Arabic Corpus Version 2", GPL-3.0, 134 stars) was last pushed 2023-07-21 with the commit "Updated Slack invite." It is a call for volunteers, not a product.
- A separate third-party iOS app "Corpus Quran" (publisher Shaila A Zerinanwar, v10.0.0, 5.0 stars from 53 ratings) packages the corpus offline: word-by-word morphology for "77,429 surface forms", "1,651 roots", verb paradigms, dependency treebanks "for all 6,236 verses", no accounts, no ads. It shows that an offline, ad-free, morphology-first app has an audience.
- Lesson: do not link or depend on qurancorpus.app anywhere in our docs or data pipeline.

### 2.4 quranmorphology.com

What it is: a one-person C# Blazor WebAssembly app (MudBlazor UI, Azure Static Web Apps, Cosmos DB), "Quran Morphology v1.1".

Does well:
- The best root page structure we saw: "ع ل م — 6th root in Quran with 854 occurrences", lemma cards with counts (عَلِمَ 382, عَلِيم 163, عِلْم 105, عالَم 73, أَعْلَم 49, عَلَّمَ 41 …), a part-of-speech breakdown (Adjective 123, Noun 306, Verb 425), and a by-surah bar chart (al-Baqarah 102).
- Lemma page: "عَلِمَ — 86th lemma in Quran with 382 occurrences", 340 verses, paginated with the full verse and a translation.
- Ayah page: every word with bilingual grammar chips (Nominative case / مرفوع, Proper noun / اسم علم), lemma and root links, and outbound links to Almaany, Ejtaal and a classical "Dictionary".
- Unique tools: pattern search (wazn), multi-root search, morphology search, Arabic text search, matching ayahs and matching phrases (LCS similarity), divine attributes, topic collections.
- Clear credits: QAC v0.4 (Dukes), the v0.5 fixes by "Mustafa" hosted on tafsir.app, Asim Iqbal's dictionary indexing, quran.com and Tanzil.

Missing or frustrating:
- Invisible to search engines: the server returns the same Blazor shell for every URL, with the same title and description, and zero Arabic characters before JavaScript runs.
- Slow: the ayah page took about 6 s to DOMContentLoaded on a fast connection (WebAssembly download).
- The viewport tag sets maximum-scale=1.0, user-scalable=no, so phone users cannot pinch-zoom small Quranic diacritics.
- English UI; no tafsir (only outbound links); no audio; the home page's embedded YouTube tutorial shows "Video player configuration error, Error 153".
- Pattern search exists but no pattern is explained.

Data source: QAC v0.5 (Mustafa's fork) plus Tanzil and quran.com text and translations. API and downloads: none.

### 2.5 quranwbw.com (not in the brief, but a direct competitor)

What it is: an open-source SvelteKit word-by-word reader (github.com/marwan/quranwbw, actively developed, last push 2026-09-29).

Does well:
- Fast: pre-generated static JSON on a Cloudflare CDN, no runtime API; pages reached DOMContentLoaded in about 0.5 s.
- Beautiful text-based Uthmani rendering with transliteration and gloss under each word, word audio, verse audio with word-timestamp highlighting, tajweed colors, 15 word-by-word languages.
- Per-word morphology page (/morphology?word=2:255:27): the QAC sentence, a mini paradigm of the root (عَلِمَ perfect, يَعْلَمُ imperfect, اِعْلَمْ imperative, عَالِم active participle, مَعْلُوْم passive participle, عِلْم verbal noun), "854 words with the same root" and "62 words appearing exactly" tables with gloss and location.
- Tafsir via the open spa5k tafsir API; AI semantic search via the Kalimat API.

Missing or frustrating:
- Client-rendered: zero Arabic characters in server HTML, no canonical tag, one generic meta description on every page.
- No root hub page, no lemma grouping (the same-root table is a flat list with "Load more"), no context in the concordance table, English UI only.
- The morphology page reuses QAC's text verbatim without an on-page attribution (credit exists only on the About page).

Data source: "our own data" built on QAC morphology; EveryAyah for verse audio. API and downloads: none documented.

### 2.6 tafsir.app — الباحث القرآني (Nuqayah)

What it is: the leading Arabic Quran research tool, part of the Nuqayah ecosystem (sunnah.one, turath.ai, muqri.com, kalimah.app, qari.app). Arabic-only UI. Mobile apps on iOS and Android.

Does well:
- Tafsir depth no one else matches: the 2:255 page links 39 tafsir works, grouped as أمّهات، جمع الأقوال، منتقاة، عامّة، موسوعات، لغة وبلاغة، معاصرة, each with author, death year and size (for example "ابن جرير الطبري (٣١٠ هـ) نحو ٢٨ مجلدًا"). Clean URLs per tafsir and verse: /tabari/2/255.
- Quran-specific lexicons: مفردات ألفاظ القرآن (الراغب)، عمدة الحفاظ (السمين الحلبي)، بصائر ذوي التمييز، المعجم الاشتقاقي المؤصل (محمد حسن جبل)، فهرس جذور كلمات القرآن، plus الصحاح، لسان العرب، القاموس المحيط، مقاييس اللغة, and i'rab works (الجدول في إعراب القرآن، إعراب الدرويش).
- The only Arabic-native root page we found: /quran-roots/علم opens "ورد «علم» في القرآن ٨٥٤ مرة" broken down in proper Arabic morphology terms ("٣٨٢ مرة فعلًا من الثلاثي المجرد"، "٤١ مرة فعلًا من باب «فَعَّلَ»"، "١٦٣ مرة اسمًا بصيغة «عَلِيم»"، "اسم فاعل"), with verses under each form.
- The etymological dictionary gives المعنى المحوري (core meaning) of each root, exactly the "explain the root clearly" layer our product needs.
- Fast, light, readable typography; canonical URL per root per dictionary in the server HTML (/ishtiqaqi/علم, /qamoos/أرض).

Missing or frustrating:
- Root and dictionary content opens as a modal over the tafsir page; the server HTML title is the generic "الباحث القرآني" (the specific title is set by JavaScript) and the root content itself loads client-side.
- No plain-language summary for non-specialists, no graph, no English, no counts per surah, no audio in the root view, and the root index is a long dense list.
- Morphology comes from the QAC v0.5 fork; data provenance per field is not shown.

Data source: digitized classical works; morphology from mustafa0x/quran-morphology (QAC v0.4 plus fixes, "Many root and lemma fixes, especially spelling issues"; the repo has no license file, last push 2022-04-18). API and downloads: none public.

### 2.7 puremonotheism.com

What it is: a Quran-only ("No sect, no tradition. Only the Quran, its language and its roots.") study site with a community forum.

Does well:
- The richest dictionary panel: eight classical lexica side by side, ordered by compiler death date, with root counts (Kitab al-Ayn 7,320; Tahdhib al-Lugha 7,873; al-Sihah 6,652; Maqayis 6,715; Mufradat 2,659; Lisan al-Arab 10,078; al-Qamus al-Muhit 10,415; Lane 7,051) and honest labels such as "earliest-disputed" on Kitab al-Ayn and "Editorial summary, not Lane's wording".
- Word click opens a side panel with surface-form chips and counts (ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ 42, ٱلصَّلَوٰةِ 9 …), "99 occurrences in the Quran", and the dictionary entries.
- Per-verse research toolbar (Questions, Grammar tags, Like, Links between verses, Roots, Translations, Related verses by shared roots, Topic, Note) and 12 translations.
- "Systems in the Quran": draggable mind-map canvases (prophets, angels, wealth, knowledge, afterlife …) with an excellent edge legend: "solid = explicit; dashed = Quranic association; dotted = inference".

Missing or frustrating:
- Ideological root claims presented as linguistics: the default translation renders الصَّلَوٰةَ in 2:3 as "hold the contact-method", and the root note states the صلو family "must remain distinct from the fire/burning family صلي". Mainstream users will distrust the site; scholars will reject it.
- Internal process text is shown to users: "manual Phase 10 Quran-context root prose audit".
- No tafsir by design.
- SEO is broken: every page has the title "Pure Monotheism" and a canonical URL pointing to the homepage, the server HTML has zero Arabic characters, and there is no per-verse URL (/quran/2/255 and /surah/2/255 both return the app's 404 page).

Data source: Tanzil text, QAC morphology (footer credit). API and downloads: none.

### 2.8 quranroots.com

What it is: a tiny Vue single-page app: pick a root from a dropdown, get a table.

Does well: very simple; the table shows part of speech, sura, ayah, transliteration, gloss and the verse fragment; it credits "The Quranic Arabic Corpus".

Missing or frustrating:
- Corrupts the Quranic text display: markup fragments are left in the verse, so words render as "<يَعْلَمُونَ>" with literal angle brackets (visible on desktop and phone).
- Undercounts: its API returned 772 rows for ع ل م where QAC has 854 (the "Nominal" group has 136 rows against 216 in QAC: عَلِيم 163 + أَعْلَم 49 + عَلَّام 4; passive participles 9 against 11).
- Rows are not in Quran order (3:167 is first), fragments only, no full verse, no tafsir, English UI, one URL for everything (no shareable root URL), zero server-rendered content.

Data source: QAC. API: undocumented JSON endpoints (/api/roots returns 1,664 entries; /api/meanings takes a POST). Downloads: none.

### 2.9 openislam.org/root_words

Does well: an alphabetical root index; each root page lists every surface form, then every verse with a translation, paginated; a sidebar lists co-occurring roots ("Appears with 802 other roots" for علم). It declares "Nothing here is copyright. No rights reserved."

Missing or frustrating: "Appears 728 times in the Quran." for علم is the verse count (QAC: 854 occurrences in 728 verses), so the label is wrong. Roots are shown hamza-stripped and unspaced (ابب, اله). Root URLs are opaque numeric IDs (/root_words/8). No viewport tag (980 px on a phone), dated design, no lemma grouping, no tafsir, no highlighting of the word in the verse.

Data source: QAC and Tanzil. API and downloads: none.

### 2.10 searchtruth.com

Does well: a long-running portal (prayer times, hadith, dictionaries, recitation); "18251 words used in the Quran" alphabetical word list; verse results with links to about 17 translations and two tafsirs (Jalalayn, King Fahad Complex); server-rendered, canonical URLs.

Missing or frustrating: misleading counts. The word list links to substring search, and "The word(s) "اتَّبَعَ" appears in 97 verse(s) in Quran." actually counts verses containing وَاتَّبَعُوا, اتَّبَعْتَ, اتُّبِعُوا and so on. No roots, no morphology, no highlighting, house ads ("Quran Teacher Free" banner), Google Analytics, 2000s design.

Data source: not stated on the pages we read. API and downloads: a downloads page and embeddable "Free Code" widgets; no API.

### 2.11 tanzil.net

Does well:
- The reference Quran text: Simple, Simple Minimal, Simple Clean, Uthmani and Uthmani Minimal, as text, XML or MySQL; version 1.1 (February 2021). Almost every other site uses it.
- Search is diacritic-insensitive, highlights matches in full verses ("582 results in 484 ayas" for علم), and has a Roots tab.

Missing or frustrating: hash routes (/#2:255, /#search/quran/علم) give a crawler one page; a fixed viewport of width=768; a 2008-era UI; letter-substring search misses root forms whose letters are not contiguous (عَالَم, مَعْلُوم) and so is not a root count.

License: "Permission is granted to copy and distribute verbatim copies of the Quran text provided here, but changing the text is not allowed", with source attribution and a link required. API: none (downloads only).

### 2.12 almaany.com (المعاني)

Analyzed from SERPs only (the site blocked all our automated clients with a Cloudflare challenge).

- It ranks in almost every Arabic SERP we sampled: جذر كلمة, معنى كلمة … في القرآن, كم مرة ذكرت, مشتقات, سجيل, الصمد.
- URL families: per word occurrence /quran/{sura}/{aya}/{word}/ titled "المعاني - سورة البقرة - آية 255 - تحليل ومعانى كُرْسِيُّهُ في القرآن الكريم, Meaning of كُرْسِيُّهُ in Holy Quran"; per word with derivatives /quran-b/{word}/ titled "الآيات والسور التى وردت فيها كلمة العلم ومشتقاتها في القرآن الكريم."; general dictionary /ar/dict/ar-ar/{word}/; a Q&A section ("سين جيم") that answers count questions.
- Snippets show a root and a short meaning sourced from Jalalayn for each word occurrence.
- Takeaway: almaany wins on breadth of templated, Arabic-titled, per-word URLs. Its weaknesses (based on titles) are a bilingual keyword-stuffed title pattern and a "word plus derivatives" concordance keyed on a surface word, not a root.

### 2.13 surahquran.com

Does well (for SEO): every verse has a static Arabic page (/aya-255-sora-2.html, about 30,000 Arabic characters of server HTML) with eight tafsirs, i'rab, translation, MP3 per verse from about 30 reciters, indexes for seven riwayat (Warsh, Qalun, Shu'ba, al-Susi, al-Duri, al-Bazzi, Qunbul), and "شرح المفردات" links to /quran-search/search/{word} pages titled "معنى يعلم في القرآن الكريم".

Missing or frustrating: five AdSense slots on a verse page and "اكتشاف المزيد" ad blocks interleaved between Quranic verses in search results; floating Telegram and WhatsApp buttons over the text; the word page is a substring search ("عدد الآيات: 201 آية" for يعلم) with unattributed glosses; no roots or morphology.

Data source: not stated; the per-word glosses are unattributed. API and downloads: per-verse MP3 and Quran PDF downloads; no API.

### 2.14 quran-words.com (تفسير كلمات القرآن)

Does well: one indexable page per word occurrence (/كلمة/يعلمون/1843) with an Arabic title "تفسير و معنى كلمة يعلمون - يُعَلِّمُونَ من سورة البقرة آية رقم 102", the verse with the word highlighted, a short gloss ("يُعَرِّفون ويُفَهِّمون"), and ScholarlyArticle JSON-LD naming an author; it ranks for "جذر كلمة يعلمون".

Missing or frustrating: the long "التفسير الاشتقاقي لجذر الكلمة" block is al-Raghib's Mufradat entry for علم reproduced verbatim with no attribution to al-Raghib or the book; the title's undiacritized word conflates يَعْلَمُونَ (know) with يُعَلِّمُونَ (teach); AdSense; no counts, no root page, no concordance.

Data source: the JSON-LD names the author بشير أحمد سليمان يونس and the site offers a PDF download centre and an Android app. API: none.

### 2.15 quran.ksu.edu.sa (مشروع المصحف الإلكتروني بجامعة الملك سعود)

Does well: an authoritative brand (King Saud University); mushaf page images with a tafsir side panel, recitations, 17 UI language options; static per-verse tafsir pages (/tafseer/tabary/sura2-aya255.html) that rank for tafsir queries such as "معنى كلمة الصمد في القرآن".

Missing or frustrating: no word-level anything (the text is page images); skeuomorphic design from the early 2010s; about 6 s to DOMContentLoaded; the reader has no viewport tag (a separate m.php exists); no canonical tags; no roots.

Data source: the university's own project ("برنامج آيات"). API and downloads: a desktop program and mobile apps; no public API.

### 2.16 e-quran.com (المصحف الإلكتروني)

Does well: a modern Arabic portal (reading, recitations, Cairo Quran radio, tafsirs, hisn al-muslim, hadith, kids, quizzes, PWA manifest), no ads observed, per-surah word-meaning pages (/w1.html … /w114.html) titled "معاني الكلمات سورة …".

Missing or frustrating: word meanings load from a JavaScript file (/js/words-v15.js) and are absent from the server HTML; our phone capture showed an empty content panel; granularity is per surah, not per word or root; no roots or morphology.

Data source: not stated. API and downloads: none found.

### 2.17 jadhr.app

What it is: "Jadhr — Arabic Root Explorer & Academy", in private beta, © 2026 Shahen Dalia, built with Lovable ("Edit with Lovable" badge). English UI.

Does well: strong positioning and marketing design (example card: ساكِن → root س ك ن → سَكَنَ، مَسْكَن، سَكِينَة، سُكُون); a three-step journey (Lookup → Academy with mnemonics and spaced repetition → Reader with tap-a-word for the Quran or Kalila wa Dimna); separate Quranic and Classical/MSA paths.

Missing or frustrating: the analysis is LLM-generated ("Outputs are generated by language and speech models and may contain errors"); our lookup of يعلمون returned "AI credits exhausted."; daily lookup limits and Paddle subscriptions; no sources; no counts; no SEO surface (generic home page only).

Data source: language models, per its terms; no sources cited in the product. API and downloads: none.

Naming risk for us: see section 10.

### 2.18 Root-family and Quran graph visualization landscape

- quranobservatory.org (Quranic Linguistics Observatory; quran.pluragate.org redirects here; GPL-3.0, created 2026-02-05, active): Next.js app with a root-to-lemma Sankey, radial surah map, arc flow, "concordance rings", dependency tree, quizzes, a good onboarding chooser ("What do you want to understand?"), Arabic/English toggle, and a power search syntax ("root:هدي pos:N"). Problems: Sankey labels are a few pixels tall (illegible Arabic); roots are displayed hamza-stripped from Buckwalter (شيا for ش ي أ, سال for س أ ل, which reads as a different root meaning "to flow"); the view is capped ("showing 50 of 1053 flows") and the root total it displays does not match the corpus (علم shows 78 in al-Baqarah, QAC has 102); it reports "71,924" tokens with no explanation.
- corpus.quran.com ontology: static PNG image maps of concepts, no interaction beyond hotspots.
- puremonotheism.com Systems: topic mind maps (draggable cards, pannable canvas, edge-type legend), Quran-only framing, not root-based.
- Ilm (arriqaaq.com/ilm): a research prototype with graph search and hadith narrator networks; not a consumer root explorer (seen in search results only, not tested).
- Conclusion: there is no root → lemma → word → verse node graph with readable word cards, no user-built topic or meaning graphs, and no publish, fork or embed flow anywhere. Our editor concept is uncontested.

## 5. Gaps no one fills well — our opportunities

Ordered by how much each would differentiate us.

1. Root, lemma and count on the word click, in Arabic, for everyone. The biggest site (quran.com) shows only gloss and transliteration; the sites that show roots are English-first, desktop-era, or dense. A plain-Arabic word card (meaning in context, root, pattern, "this root appears 854 times in 728 verses", "see all") is the core unmet need of the casual audience.
2. Trustworthy, defined counts with sources. Section 3 shows the web gives contradictory numbers and never says what was counted. Owning "كم مرة ذكرت كلمة … في القرآن" with exact definitions, dataset version and a "why other sites differ" note wins both trust and featured answers.
3. Pattern (wazn) explanation in context. Several sites label patterns (form II, باب «فَعَّلَ», صيغة «عَلِيم») and quranmorphology has pattern search, but nobody explains what the pattern does to the meaning (فَعِيل and فَعَّال as intensive forms, مُفَعَّل as the form-II passive participle) on the word itself or on a pattern hub page. This is the bridge from "root" to "understanding" that the brief asks for.
4. Field-level provenance and visible disagreement. Most sites cite nothing, copy classical text without attribution (quran-words.com), or generate with AI (jadhr.app). Even verified corpora disagree on roots for about 15% of words (QAMAR measured 84.57% root agreement with QAC over the full Quran). Showing the source and version of every root, lemma and gloss, and showing when sources disagree, is unoccupied ground and the main thing scholars need.
5. An honest root-family graph. No one ships a readable node canvas from root to verse, and the existing charts mislead (truncated totals, hamza-stripped roots, unreadable labels). We can also be the first to separate "shares root letters" from "shares meaning": root س ج ل groups سِجِلّ (a scroll, 21:104) with سِجِّيل (baked clay, 11:82, 15:74, 105:4), which classical lexicographers describe as a loanword; a graph that draws them as semantic cousins teaches something false.
6. One flow from word to root to every verse to tafsir. Today the user hops between corpus.quran.com (root), quran.com (tafsir) and tafsir.app (lexicons). Clicking any occurrence in our concordance and reading its tafsir inline, without leaving the root page, is a workflow nobody offers.
7. User-built topic and meaning graphs with publish, fork and embed. Only puremonotheism has topic maps, editor-curated and sect-framed. Community-built, citable, forkable graphs are new.
8. A modern Arabic interactive concordance in the المعجم المفهرس tradition. The standard printed concordances exist only as PDFs. An alphabetical root index with every occurrence, KWIC, filters (form, POS, surah, Makki/Madani) and export is a scholar magnet and an SEO surface.
9. Indexable Arabic root, lemma and word pages. The data-rich sites are SPAs with zero server-rendered Arabic; the SERPs are filled by ad-heavy templated pages and Q&A farms. Server-rendered, well-titled, fast, ad-free pages for about 1,650 roots, a few thousand lemmas and 77,429 word positions would compete directly.
10. A root-centric open dataset and public API with a clear license, versioning and a changelog. QAC forbids modification of its file, QUL has no API, the Quran Foundation API needs credentials and has no morphology fields documented, and no one offers roots, lemmas, patterns, counts and concordance through an open API.
11. Mobile-first scholarly depth. Corpus and openislam do not fit a phone, tanzil is fixed at 768 px, KSU forks to a separate mobile site, quranmorphology blocks zoom. Deep data that reads well at 390 px is rare.
12. Learning by root family, free and grounded. jadhr.app sells spaced repetition by root but on AI data behind a paywall; the Observatory has quizzes. A free review deck driven by verified root families, with "in context" drills, has no serious competitor.
13. A cross-corpus root index for the ecosystem (later). When hadith, dictionary and books come online, the same root hub can show Quran occurrences next to hadith and classical usage. No competitor is positioned to do this; tafsir.app's sister sites are separate products without a shared root index.

## 6. Mistakes and anti-patterns to avoid

Respect for the Quranic text:
- Never inject markup or code artifacts into verse text (quranroots.com shows "<يَعْلَمُونَ>" with literal angle brackets).
- Never place ads near, around, or between verses (surahquran.com interleaves ad blocks between verses in search results and runs five AdSense slots per verse page; quran-words.com, holyquran.net and searchtruth.com also run ads). Our rule: zero ads, anywhere.
- Do not render Quran words as images when text will do (corpus word-by-word pages), and if a page-glyph font is used for mushaf fidelity, keep a correct Unicode layer for selection, copy and screen readers.
- Do not generate an SEO or accessibility text layer by naive normalization: quran.com's hidden H1 reads "لا الاه الا هو … ولا ييوده". Use Tanzil's Simple Clean text, which keeps correct spelling without diacritics.
- Do not show Arabic at sizes no one can read (the Observatory Sankey), and do not lock zoom (quranmorphology's user-scalable=no).
- Never mix internal workflow text into user-facing content (puremonotheism's "manual Phase 10 Quran-context root prose audit").

Misleading data and claims:
- Never let a language model assign roots, lemmas or meanings without verified data underneath. Measured LLM root accuracy on Quranic words is 21.58% (GPT-4o) and 44.09% (LLaMA-4) on the first 10,000 words (QAMAR, AbjadNLP 2026); jadhr.app ships exactly this.
- Never label a count without saying what it counts: openislam calls 728 verses "728 times"; searchtruth's "appears in 97 verses" includes other words; the Observatory's root total changes with how many flows are shown.
- Never treat letter-substring search as a root or word count (tanzil, surahquran, searchtruth).
- Never display Buckwalter roots by naive transliteration: QAC writes hamza in roots as "A" (quranroots' API maps "Abb" to أ ب ب); converting "A" to ا produces شيا and سال, which reads as different roots.
- Never present a sectarian or idiosyncratic reading as the linguistic meaning of a root (puremonotheism's "contact-method" for الصلاة). Where interpretations differ, show the mainstream tafsir view first, label alternatives, and cite.
- Do not imply that words sharing root letters share meaning when the sources say otherwise (loanwords, homonymous roots, proper nouns). Proper nouns and loanwords (إبراهيم, إستبرق, سجيل) need a "no Arabic root" or "معرّب" state rather than a forced family.
- Always attribute quoted classical text (quran-words.com reproduces al-Raghib's Mufradat entry without naming it), and attribute upstream data on the page where it is used (quranwbw's morphology page does not).

Product and UX:
- No paywalls or daily limits on basic lookups (jadhr.app's "Sign in … to unlock more daily lookups" and "AI credits exhausted.").
- No clutter: floating chat buttons over the text, keyword-stuffed bilingual titles, multi-tafsir walls on one page with no structure.
- Do not build a heavy client-only app for content pages (Blazor WebAssembly took about 6 s; every SPA in this study is invisible to non-JS crawlers).
- Do not depend on volunteer domains or idle upstream projects without a fallback (qurancorpus.app lapsed; the QAC v2 repo has been idle since 2023).

### 7.5 Implications for our pages (proposal, to be finalized in the SEO and IA docs)

- Server-render every content page (root hub, lemma, word occurrence, verse, tafsir, pattern hub, concordance view) with the Arabic content in the initial HTML; hydrate the interactive parts.
- Use Arabic-script slugs on the Arabic locale, which is what almaany, quran-words.com and tafsir.app do, with an English mirror under /en/ and hreflang pairs. For example: /جذر/علم (root hub), /جذر/علم/عليم (lemma), /كلمة/2/255/27 (word occurrence), /آية/2/255 (verse), /تفسير/الميسر/2/255, /صيغة/فعيل (pattern hub). Never use Buckwalter codes or numeric IDs in public URLs.
- Title templates that match the query families, in Arabic first: "جذر «علم» في القرآن الكريم: ورد ٨٥٤ مرة في ٧٢٨ آية — المشتقات والمعاني"; "معنى «يَعْلَمُ» في آية الكرسي (البقرة ٢٥٥): الجذر والوزن والإعراب"; "صيغة «فَعِيل» في القرآن الكريم: أمثلتها ودلالتها". Test Arabic-Indic against Western digits in titles; keep Western digits in URLs.
- Put the count answer in the first paragraph of the root hub, as a sentence a search engine can lift ("وردت مادة «ع ل م» في القرآن الكريم ٨٥٤ مرة في ٧٢٨ آية، بأربع عشرة صيغة …"), followed by the definitions table and the "why other sites say 856 or 750" note.
- Structured data: BreadcrumbList everywhere; DefinedTerm and DefinedTermSet for roots, lemmas and patterns; Dataset for the open data page; Article for curated root essays. Do not count on FAQ rich results, which search engines now restrict, but keep question-shaped headings.
- Internal linking is the moat: every word links to its root hub, lemma and pattern; every root hub links to all lemmas, the concordance, the graph and the top verses; every verse links to its words. This is roughly 1,650 roots × lemmas × 77,429 positions of crawlable, non-thin pages, as long as each page carries unique content (context gloss, counts, tafsir excerpt) rather than a template shell.

## 8. Recommendations ranked by impact

1. Build the verified data spine first, with provenance on every field. Store each root, lemma, pattern, gloss and count with its source and version (QAC v0.4, the mustafa0x v0.5 fixes, QUL morphology, QAMAR once released, classical lexica), keep upstream files untouched, and apply our corrections as a separate, reviewable patch layer. Show disagreements in the UI ("المصادر تختلف"). Never generate roots or meanings with a language model; use AI at most for search and summarization over cited data, clearly labelled. Why: trust is the market gap (sections 3, 5.2, 5.4), and the licenses require it (section 9).
2. Ship the word card as the product's heart: one click on any word gives, in plain Arabic, the meaning in this verse, the root with its core meaning (المعنى المحوري), the pattern and what it adds, "this root: N occurrences in M verses; this lemma: K", the form's part of speech, word audio, and buttons to the root hub, the concordance, the graph and the verse's tafsir. Progressive disclosure for scholars (full segment tags, i'rab, lexicon entries, source versions). Why: this is exactly what quran.com lacks and what casual users search for.
3. Launch server-rendered Arabic root hubs, lemma pages and word-occurrence pages with the URL and title patterns in 7.5. Why: these query families have high volume and weak, contradictory incumbents; the data-rich competitors are invisible to crawlers.
4. Make counts a feature: a definitions table on every root hub (root occurrences, verses, per lemma, per exact form, per surah, Makki/Madani), the dataset version, and a public "how we count" page that reconciles common numbers (854 against 856, 728, 750). Why: it wins "كم مرة" queries and earns scholarly trust.
5. Build the root-family graph canvas to the standard competitors miss: readable Arabic cards (word, root, location, verse fragment), edges typed and styled by evidence (derivation, same lemma, same pattern, editorial association, user association, in the spirit of puremonotheism's solid/dashed/dotted legend), explicit flags for loanwords, homonymous roots and proper nouns, server-rendered permalinks, publish, fork and embed. Why: uncontested, and it is the brand.
6. Ship a KWIC concordance in the المعجم المفهرس tradition: root → forms → every occurrence with the word highlighted in context, filters (lemma, form, POS, person, surah, Makki/Madani, juz), sort, word audio per row, inline tafsir per row, CSV and citation export. Why: scholars and students need it, and the printed standards exist only as PDFs.
7. Treat text fidelity as non-negotiable: Tanzil or QPC Unicode text, a proper Quranic font (KFGQPC Uthmanic Hafs or similar), real selectable and copyable text, correct copy with a citation, no markup inside verses, no ads anywhere, no floating widgets over the text, accessible zoom and generous Arabic sizes on phones. Why: this is where several competitors lose credibility.
8. Integrate multiple tafsirs with metadata the way tafsir.app does (author, death year, type: مأثور، لغوي، فقهي، مختصر، معاصر), Arabic first, with licensing checked per work, and open any verse's tafsir inline from any concordance row. Why: removes the hop between three sites.
9. Add pattern (صيغة/وزن) hub pages and per-word pattern explanations: what فَعِيل, فَعَّال, مُفْعِل, اسْتَفْعَلَ … contribute to meaning, with Quranic examples and counts. Why: unique pedagogy and an SEO surface ("وزن كلمة …") currently held by thin scrapers.
10. Publish an open dataset and a public, documented API (versioned, with a changelog and citation formats), respecting upstream terms: verbatim upstream files plus our separately licensed layers (patterns, glosses, counts, graphs, corrections). Why: no root-centric open API exists, and developers and researchers will cite and link us.
11. Mobile-first, local-first study features: notes, bookmarks, collections, saved trails and graphs, and a free spaced-repetition deck that drills root families "in context". Why: matches quran.com's personal layer and beats jadhr.app's paywalled, AI-based academy on trust and price.
12. A correction workflow (propose on a specific field → two reviewers → merge with credit → public changelog). Why: corpus.quran.com proved the community will submit corrections; its message boards are stale and nothing modern replaces them.
13. Performance budget and PWA offline for the reader, the word card and recently viewed roots. Why: quranwbw's static-JSON speed (about 0.5 s) sets user expectations, and the Corpus Quran app shows demand for offline morphology.
14. Plan the ecosystem around a shared root index from day one (Quran now; dictionary, hadith and books later), so every future corpus attaches to the same root hubs. Why: a long-term moat no competitor is structured for.

## 9. Licensing facts that shape the data plan

- QAC morphology v0.4: GPL with "copy and distribute verbatim copies … but CHANGING IT IS NOT ALLOWED", source attribution and a link to corpus.quran.com required; download needs an email. Practical approach (also used by mustafa0x/quran-morphology): ship the upstream file verbatim and apply our fixes as a separate patch layer.
- Tanzil text: verbatim copies only, attribution and a link to tanzil.net required.
- QUL (Tarteel): downloads in SQLite and JSON intended "to download and package with your own project"; no API; check each resource's own license before redistribution through our API.
- Quran Foundation API: app credentials and their terms; better as a reference than as a runtime dependency, given the brief's own-database preference.
- Classical lexica (Lane, Lisan, Qamus, Maqayis, Sihah, Mufradat): the works are public domain, but specific digitizations and editions may carry rights; record the source edition for each.
- Modern works such as محمد حسن جبل's المعجم الاشتقاقي المؤصل (author died 1436 AH / 2015) and the Cairo Academy's معجم ألفاظ القرآن الكريم are in copyright; license them or link to them, do not ingest them.
- QAMAR (Faqihi, Bouzoubaa, Tajmout, Namly, AbjadNLP 2026): "will be released as an open-source resource … following the completion of the peer-review process"; worth tracking as a second verified source for cross-checking roots and lemmas.
